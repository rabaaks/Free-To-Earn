01 / Our mission

Freedom to work.
Freedom to earn.

A first job can be a first step: income, experience, new skills, and a path toward better opportunities.

Free to Earn is a conservative political interest group advocating for the elimination of government-imposed minimum-wage requirements. We believe people should be able to negotiate compensation that reflects their circumstances and the needs of their community.

Our group chose this cause because employment policy affects students entering the workforce, workers seeking flexibility, and businesses deciding whether they can hire. We want to put more voluntary work opportunities within reach.

We support preserving workplace protections against discrimination, unsafe conditions, and other unlawful practices.

The issue

A wage floor cannot
guarantee a job.

Minimum-wage laws set the lowest legal rate for covered work. They do not ensure that every person who wants work will find an employer able to offer a job.

We believe a single mandated wage floor can affect different workers, businesses, and communities differently. A small business and a large corporation may have different resources; a new worker and an experienced employee may bring different skills to a role.

When hiring costs change, businesses may adjust hiring, hours, prices, or how work gets done. The effects vary by situation. We believe workers and employers should have more room to negotiate.

02 / Research and facts

Different systems.
Useful questions.

A

International examples

Eight OECD countries do not have a statutory national minimum wage: Austria, Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Italy, Norway, Sweden, and Switzerland.

All eight are classified as having very high human development in the UNDP's 2025 report, which uses 2023 data. In these countries, collective bargaining agreements set wage floors for large parts of the workforce.

HDI combines measures of health, education, and income. UNDP Human Development Report 2025, data for 2023.

B

U.S. snapshot

In 2024, about 82,000 hourly workers earned exactly the federal minimum wage; another 760,000 earned below it.

BLS notes these figures do not establish whether each worker was covered by federal, state, or local minimum-wage rules. Many states and cities require a higher rate.

BLS annual averages, 2024. Figures are rounded. This is a count of hourly workers, not all workers.

03 / What we support

More room to
work and advance.

01

Remove statutory wage floors

Eliminate minimum-wage requirements at the federal, state, and local levels.

02

Protect freedom to negotiate

Give workers and employers greater flexibility to agree on compensation that fits the role and circumstances.

03

Keep essential protections

Preserve workplace-safety and antidiscrimination protections, enforce agreements, and address unlawful practices.

04

Help people grow

Encourage clear job offers, access to wage information, training, and opportunities to build skills.

Our view: a legal wage floor cannot guarantee employment. We believe a more flexible labor market can open more ways for people to begin working and advance.

04 / Campus outreach

Bring the conversation
to campus.

Students are preparing for first jobs, internships, and part-time work. Employment policy affects their options.

Free to Earn would host permitted information tables in campus common areas, distribute flyers, offer branded stickers or T-shirts, and invite students to join. We would also hold open discussions about hiring costs, job opportunities, and wage flexibility.
